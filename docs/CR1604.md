# Mackie CR1604-VLZ

The third mixer in the switcher ("Mackie 1604"), modelled on Chapman's CR1604-VLZ from its owner's manual. Unlike Mixer A and Mixer B, which are two skins of one generic mixer, the 1604 is its own hardware: its own state, level model, audio graph and surface.

| Layer | File |
|---|---|
| State, validation, level model | `js/cr1604.js` |
| Rear panel (jacks) | `DEVICE_TYPES.cr1604` in `js/connection-model.js` |
| Audio graph | `js/graph-1604.js` (the generic mixer's is `js/graph-generic.js`) |
| Surface | `js/ui/mixer-1604-view.js`, skin `mackie1604` in `js/mixer-models.js` |
| Signal-flow explainer | `renderFlow1604` in `js/ui/flow.js` |
| Tests | `tests/cr1604.test.mjs` |

A state says which mixer it belongs to with `state.model` (`"cr1604"`; no `model` means the generic mixer). `computeMix`, `createMixerState`, `MixerStore` and the audio engine dispatch on it, so the scenario conditions and the listening bar read either mixer. Switching to or from the 1604 rebuilds the current scenario on the new hardware. The engine swaps only the mixer graph: the stems keep playing on one timeline.

## What is modelled

Page numbers are from the owner's manual.

**Channels 1–16** (pp. 10–21). Each channel has its own controls:

- **Inputs.** A MIC jack (XLR, phantom from the single rear PHANTOM switch) and a LINE jack (¼″). Only one can be used per channel.
- **TRIM.** +10…+60 dB through MIC. LINE is padded 20 dB, so the same knob reads −10…+40 dB there.
- **LOW CUT.** 75 Hz at 18 dB/octave.
- **EQ.** LOW is a shelf at 80 Hz. MID is a peaking band with 1.5-octave bandwidth that sweeps 100 Hz–8 kHz. HI is a shelf at 12 kHz. Each band is ±15 dB.
- **AUX knobs.** AUX 1 and AUX 2 have a PRE switch. AUX 3 and AUX 4 have a 5/6 SHIFT switch.
- **PAN, MUTE, SOLO.** PAN is equal-power.
- **Assigns.** 1-2, 3-4 and L-R.
- **Fader**, plus the OL (MUTE) and −20 (SOLO) LEDs.

**Tap points**, as the manual describes them:

| Signal | Taken | Follows |
|---|---|---|
| AUX 1/2 with PRE down | after LOW CUT | TRIM, LOW CUT only, not EQ, MUTE or fader |
| AUX 1/2 post, AUX 3–6 | after the fader, before PAN | EQ, MUTE, fader |
| SOLO, LEVEL SET (PFL) | after EQ, before MUTE and fader | keeps working while muted |
| SOLO, NORMAL (AFL) | after PAN | as the channel's assigns hear it |
| L-R, 1-2, 3-4 | after PAN; odd = left, even = right | everything |

MUTE cuts L-R, the subgroups and every post send. PRE sends and PFL solo carry on.

**Output section** (pp. 22–27):

- **AUX SENDS.** Masters and solo switches exist for 1 and 2 only. Sends 3–6 leave at unity.
- **SUB 1–4.** Faders with ASSIGN TO MAIN MIX L/R. A subgroup on both sides drops 3 dB per side.
- **MAIN L-R MIX fader.** Feeds the MAIN OUTS and MONO OUT (L + R through MONO LEVEL: U at about 1:30, +6 dB at the top).
- **TAPE IN.** A stereo RCA input with a level knob (U at the detent, +20 dB), TAPE TO MAIN MIX, and TAPE in the SOURCE matrix. A laptop plugs in here with a 3.5 mm ↔ RCA cable.
- **C-R/PHONES.** The SOURCE matrix (MAIN MIX, SUBS 1-2, SUBS 3-4, TAPE) through the C-R/PHONES knob. Any SOLO replaces the selection, through the SOLO knob. MODE switches between NORMAL (AFL) and LEVEL SET (PFL), with the LEVEL SET LED and the RUDE SOLO LIGHT.
- **Meters.** Two 12-LED columns read the SOURCE mix, or the solo, before any level knob. PFL solo shows on the left meter only.

**Knob tapers** (state stores positions 0…1):

- Faders: U three quarters up, +10 dB at the top (same as the generic mixer).
- Channel AUX knobs: U at the centre detent, +15 dB at the top.
- AUX masters, C-R/PHONES, SOLO: U at the detent, +10 dB at the top.

**Listening.** The bar offers MAIN (main outs and mono), each aux or subgroup that has a speaker patched to it, and PHONES. PHONES is the engineer's C-R/PHONES: the SOURCE mix, or the solo.

## Simplifications

- **Not modelled yet:** channel inserts, direct outs (channels 1–8), the four stereo aux returns (with EFFECTS TO MONITORS, the AUX RET 3/4 options and RETURNS SOLO), tape out and the main insert. The lab has no effects processors or recorders to plug into them. Add them together with those devices.
- **Level model.** `computeMix` (used by scenario checks) leaves EQ and LOW CUT out of its level estimates. The audio and the LEDs include them.
- **Pan.** The real board's pan is "constant loudness" with about 4 dB of centre dip. The lab uses equal power (3 dB), like the generic mixer.
- **Meter scale.** The meter labels are Mackie's dBu scale, with 0 dBu at −18 dBFS. CLIP lights at −2 dBFS.
- **Laptop input.** The stereo laptop can only use TAPE IN. A ¼″ breakout into two LINE jacks (left and right on an odd/even pair, as the manual suggests) isn't modelled.

## Scenarios

The numbered scenarios are written for the generic mixer: their hints name input 9/10, and they assume pre-fader aux sends. On the 1604 the scenario list shows Free play only. The 1604's Free play builds the same gig:
- mics go into the MIC jacks and the keys into a LINE jack;
- PHANTOM is on;
- PRE is down on every channel and LOW CUT is on everything except drums and bass, as the manual recommends;
- every channel is assigned to L-R;
- SOLO is in LEVEL SET (PFL) mode;
- the laptop is in TAPE IN, off the main mix.

The 1604's own scenarios are still to be written, with the course owner.
